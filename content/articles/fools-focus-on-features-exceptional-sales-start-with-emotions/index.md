## THE ORIGIN STORY

I recently moved from Roosevelt Island to the East Village. Along with a new apartment comes a new grocery store, a new coffee shop, and most importantly, a new gym.

For me, the gym is more than a gym.

It's where I go when I'm in a bad mood and need an environment that keeps me away from destructive habits. It's where I go when I want to be around people without having to actively socialize. And strangely enough, it's where I do my best writing. I wrote an entire book manuscript on the gym floor of the Crunch Fitness in Kips Bay.

So choosing the right gym matters.

Yesterday, I spent about six hours touring every gym within walking distance of my new apartment. Consider it my own version of an investment banking Superday. I wanted to answer one question:

**Where do I want to go every day for the next year of my life?**

The gyms themselves were exactly what you'd expect.

Blink Fitness was cheap and utilitarian.

Crunch varied dramatically by location.

TMPL offered premium aesthetics and amenities.

Equinox had the strongest brand and the highest price tag.

## THE STRATEGY

What interested me wasn't the facilities.

It was the sales process.

Every salesperson I met made the same mistake.

They assumed they already knew what I cared about.

Within minutes, I was hearing about premium locker rooms, complimentary towels, and shaving cream.

One rep spent several minutes explaining their shaving cream.

I don't shave at the gym.

I don't give a shit about shaving cream.

And that's when I realized something:

Every salesperson started with features.

Not one started with discovery.

Nobody asked why I was looking for a gym.

Nobody asked what role it played in my life.

Nobody asked what I was actually trying to buy.

The clearest example came when I asked every gym the same question:

**"How crowded does this place get during peak hours?"**

Every salesperson gave the same answer:

"Don't worry. You'll always find an open machine."

They assumed crowded was a bug. But for me, it was a feature.

For me, crowded was a benefit.

I like energy. I like momentum. I like being surrounded by people trying to improve themselves.

Without realizing it, they were actively selling **against my preferences.**

Because they skipped discovery, they never understood the outcome I was trying to create.

Gartner estimates that roughly 40% of B2B buying decisions end in "no decision."

I understand why.

I still haven't chosen a gym.

Not because the options were bad.

Because nobody gave me a compelling emotional reason to choose them.

## THE TAKEAWAY

Fools lead with features.

Exceptional salespeople lead with emotions.

Run discovery first. Understand what the customer is actually trying to achieve. Identify the emotional outcome they're chasing.

Then, and only then, start talking about the shaving cream.
