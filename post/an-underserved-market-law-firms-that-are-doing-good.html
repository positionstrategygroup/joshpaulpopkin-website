An Underserved Market: Law Firms That Are Doing Good

Nearly every AEO agency founder I've spoken with is focused on B2B SaaS companies. But I've become increasingly interested in a different market.

By Josh Paul PopkinNovember 12, 2026 · 3 min read

We are at the beginning of the AI search era.

For years, the way people discovered businesses was relatively straightforward. A prospective client would search Google, review the results on the first page, click through a handful of websites, and use that information to decide whom to contact.

That customer journey is changing.

More people are turning to AI platforms like ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity to research their options, ask follow-up questions and narrow their choices before ever visiting a company's website.

Instead of reviewing ten different websites, a prospective client can describe their situation to an AI assistant and receive a shortlist of professionals who may be able to help.

For businesses that depend on being discovered by new clients, I believe this represents one of the most significant shifts in marketing in decades.

As I build Position Strategy Group, an Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) firm, I spend a lot of time talking with others in the industry. Friends, colleagues and even competitors. I love learning what they're building and how they're helping companies navigate AI search.

One thing I've noticed is that nearly every AEO agency founder I've spoken with is focused on B2B SaaS companies.

And that makes sense.

Technology startups often have significant growth ambitions, marketing budgets backed by venture capital, and products that can be difficult for prospective customers to understand. AI search presents a compelling opportunity for these businesses.

But I've become increasingly interested in a different market.

Founder-led law firms whose attorneys genuinely care about their work and the people they represent.

Over the past several months, I've had conversations with attorneys who have dedicated their careers to representing people facing some of the most difficult circumstances of their lives.

These are professionals who have spent years developing expertise in their respective fields. They care about achieving good outcomes for their clients, take pride in their work, and believe deeply in the importance of what they do.

Of course, they also want to build successful and profitable businesses. I don't see those objectives as contradictory.

What interests me is that many of these attorneys have extraordinary experience and compelling stories, yet relatively limited visibility among the people who could benefit from their services.

Historically, law firms have relied heavily on referrals, traditional advertising and Google search to attract new clients.

But as prospective clients increasingly turn to AI systems for guidance, the way attorneys are discovered and evaluated is changing.

Imagine someone looking for an attorney after experiencing a serious injury, workplace discrimination or an assault. They may not know which type of lawyer they need, let alone which individual attorney is best equipped to help them.

Increasingly, I believe those individuals will use AI assistants to understand their situation and identify potential legal representation.

The question for law firms is whether their experience, expertise and track record will be accurately represented when those conversations happen.

This is where I see a significant opportunity for Answer Engine Optimization.

The objective isn't simply to generate more website traffic. It's to help deserving professionals become more visible to the people who are actively looking for their expertise.

I don't believe this market is necessarily larger than B2B SaaS. But I do believe the work has the potential to be more personally meaningful.

Helping a technology company acquire another customer can be valuable. Helping an attorney connect with someone who needs representation can have an impact that extends far beyond a commercial transaction.

That's the kind of work I'm increasingly interested in doing.

As I continue building Position, I'll share what I'm learning about AI search, the challenges facing independent law firms, and the impact this work has on the professionals and clients we serve.

It's an exciting time to be in marketing, and I'm particularly excited about the opportunity to work with people who believe deeply in what they do.
